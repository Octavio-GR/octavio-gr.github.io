Identity

Who I Am

I have worked in Customer Support, Technical Support, and Operations for over 10 years. My job has consistently been to sit between frustrated users and complex infrastructure, translating technical problems into concrete actions.

During my time handling high-volume support queues, I noticed a pattern: most operational systems drift. They accumulate exceptions, undocumented workarounds, and tribal knowledge until only the original creators can navigate them.

Because of this, my professional focus is always on structure. Whether I am writing a root-cause analysis, establishing a handoff procedure between shifts, or documenting a workaround, my goal is to build and support systems that don't rely on my personal memory to function.

What I Do

I troubleshoot SaaS platforms, hardware, and networks, primarily for U.S.-based customers. Working inside strict KPI and SLA-bound environments means resolution isn't just about finding a quick fix. It is about fixing the issue within the standard time, documenting the root cause, and ensuring the next agent can verify the solution.

I am bilingual (English/Spanish), which allows me to handle escalations and bridge the gap between technical infrastructure and non-technical users across different accounts and teams.

Thinking & Purpose

How I Think

I default to documentation over memory. Human memory is unreliable under time and stress; a written decision is not. If a technical choice, a tradeoff, or a dependency isn't written down, I treat it as if it doesn't exist yet.

I think in systems, not in isolated tickets. If a device syncing error happens once, it's a ticket; if it happens fifty times, it's a systemic friction point that needs a documented root cause, not just another quick fix. That is the lens I apply whether I'm looking at a support workflow, a recurring incident, or a web architecture.

What I Care About

Five things, in this order (These are operational constraints, not aesthetic preferences):

  • Structure before decoration.
  • Evidence before opinion.
  • Observation before conclusion.
  • Documentation before memory.
  • Systems before pages.

Why Toroide Exists

In technical support, a claim without evidence is useless. If an escalation doesn't have logs, steps to reproduce, or documentation, it gets rejected.

Toroide exists to apply that exact same standard to a professional portfolio. Instead of writing paragraphs asking a recruiter or engineering manager to trust my marketing claims, Toroide is built so they can inspect my logic directly. The repository, the static architecture, and the documentation are the evidence.
